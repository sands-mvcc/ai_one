Demystifying Debits & Credits

Welcome to Accounting 101, class! I am your interactive instructor. In banking, you hear "credit" and think plus or "debit card" and think minus. Forget everything you think you know about those terms right now. In financial accounting, they mean something beautifully simple.

Test Your Reflexes First

The Supreme Golden Rule:

Debit means LEFT.
Credit means RIGHT.

That is literally it. Repeat it out loud. They do not mean good, bad, add, or subtract. They only indicate structural sides of a ledger page.

💡

Fun Fact: "Debit" comes from Latin debere (what is due), and "Credit" from Latin credere (what is trusted). In ledger styling, they just represent the Columns!

Module 2: Fundamental Equation

The Sacred Balance Scale

Every single transaction a company makes must leave the universe in perfect balance. This is represented by the legendary Accounting Equation:

Assets = Liabilities + Equity

If you add an Asset (left side of equation), you must either decrease another Asset or add to the Liabilities or Equity (right side of equation). Let's play with this equation visually using our interactive hydraulic scale!

Module 3: Visual Mnemonics

Deciphering the "DEALER" Formula

How do we know which side increases or decreases an account? We use the globally trusted academic acronym D-E-A-L-E-R. Let's break it in two halves!

Increased by DEBIT (Left)

D - E - A

These account types are naturally structural LEFT-side players. Adding to them requires a Debit; subtracting requires a Credit.

D

Drawings / Withdrawals

Value removed by owners for personal use.

E

Expenses

The transactional cost of running operational affairs (e.g., rent, wages).

A

Assets

Value resources owned by the business (e.g., cash, land, equipment).

Increased by CREDIT (Right)

L - E - R

These account types are naturally structural RIGHT-side players. Adding to them requires a Credit; subtracting requires a Debit.

L

Liabilities

Debts/claims held by external creditors (e.g., accounts payable).

E

Equity

The ultimate net residual interest belonging to the business owner.

R

Revenue

Earnings realized through commercial activities/sales.
